The Rāshidūn Caliphs

632–661 CE 11–40 AH

The four "Rightly Guided" caliphs who succeeded the Prophet Muḥammad. They ruled from Medina (and later Kufa) over the rapidly expanding Islamic state.

# Ruler Accession (AH) Accession (CE) End (CE) Notes
1 Abū Bakr aṣ-Ṣiddīq 12 Rabīʿ I, 11 8 June 632 23 August 634 ʿAbdallāh b. ʿUthmān. Died naturally.
2 ʿUmar I al-Fārūq 22 Jumādā II, 13 23 August 634 3 November 644 ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb. Assassinated.
3 ʿUthmān Dhū l-Nūrayn 1 Muḥarram, 24 11 November 644 17 June 656 ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān. Assassinated.
4 ʿAlī I al-Murtaḍā 18 Dhū l-Ḥijja, 35 17 June 656 28 January 661 ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib. Assassinated.

The Umayyad Caliphs

661–750 CE 41–132 AH

The Umayyad dynasty, descended from Umayya b. ʿAbd Shams, ruled from Damascus. They presided over the largest territorial expansion of the Islamic state.

5 ʿAbd al-Malik 65 April 685 October 705 Marwanid. Builder of the Dome of the Rock.

The Abbasid Caliphs

750–1258 CE 132–656 AH

The Abbasid dynasty, descended from al-ʿAbbās b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib, the Prophet's uncle, ruled from Baghdad (after 762). Their caliphate ended with the Mongol sack of Baghdad in 1258.

The Umayyads of Córdoba

756–1031 CE 138–422 AH

ʿAbd al-Raḥmān I, a surviving Umayyad prince, fled the Abbasid revolution and established an independent emirate in al-Andalus. His descendants ruled as emirs (756–929) and then as caliphs (929–1031) from Córdoba.
